How to plan meals for guests without the stress

How many dishes to cook, how much to make per person, and a timeline that gets everything to the table hot.

By the What2Eat team · Published 17 November 2026 · 9 min read

  2. Step 2: Build a balanced menu
  3. Step 3: Work out quantities
  4. Step 4: Make a timeline

Cooking for guests is where meal planning really earns its keep. Whether it's a family dawat, an Eid lunch or a dinner with friends, the stress usually comes from three questions: what to cook, how much to cook, and how to get it all ready on time. Here's how to answer each.

Step 1: Decide how many dishes

More dishes doesn't mean a better dawat. A balanced menu matters more than a long one. As a rough guide:

GuestsMain dishesPlus
4 to 62Rice or bread, raita, salad, 1 sweet
8 to 123Rice dish, bread, raita, salad, 1 to 2 sweets
15 to 254 to 5Rice dish, bread, 2 sides, 2 sweets

Step 2: Build a balanced menu

Pick dishes that are different from each other in three ways:

  • Protein: don't serve three chicken dishes. Mix chicken, beef or mutton, and a vegetarian option such as daal or a vegetable dish.
  • Texture: combine something with gravy (karahi, korma), something dry (kababs, tikka), and a rice dish (pulao or biryani).
  • Richness: balance one heavy dish with something fresh, like raita, salad or a light daal.

A classic Pakistani guest menu for 8 to 10 people might be chicken karahi, beef pulao, shami kababs, raita, salad, naan, and kheer or gulab jamun.

Step 3: Work out quantities

Appetites vary, but these per-person figures are a sensible starting point when serving several dishes:

  • Meat in curries: about 150 to 200 g raw meat per person across all meat dishes.
  • Rice: about 75 to 100 g uncooked rice per person if rice is a main dish.
  • Bread: one and a half to two naan or roti per person.
  • Dessert: one generous portion each, with a little extra.

Cook about 10% extra. Running out is worse than having leftovers, and leftovers can be turned into tomorrow's meals.

Step 4: Make a timeline

Two days before

  • Finalise the menu and shopping list. Buy dry goods and meat.

The day before

  • Marinate meat. Make ginger-garlic paste and fried onions.
  • Cook dishes that improve overnight: korma, nihari, haleem, shami kabab mixture.
  • Make the dessert if it's served cold, such as kheer, custard or trifle.

On the day

  • Morning: chop salad vegetables, make raita, prepare rice.
  • Three hours before: start the longest-cooking dish.
  • One hour before: cook the rice dish and fry kababs.
  • Last 20 minutes: karahi or anything best served fresh, and warm the bread.

Hosting tips that save the day

  1. Ask about dietary needs early. Allergies, vegetarian guests and spice levels are easier to plan for in advance.
  2. Cook what you know. A dawat isn't the time to try a new recipe for the first time.
  3. Use make-ahead dishes. The more that's done before guests arrive, the more time you spend with them.
  4. Clean as you go. An empty sink before guests arrive makes serving much easier.
  5. Set up a serving table. Lay out dishes, spoons and plates an hour early.

Quick answers

How many dishes should I make for 10 guests?

Three main dishes, a rice dish, bread, raita, salad and one or two sweets is a comfortable spread for about ten people.

What can I cook a day ahead for guests?

Korma, nihari, haleem, kabab mixtures, most curries and cold desserts like kheer and trifle.
