What should I cook today? A simple way to decide in five minutes

A five-question method that turns “I don't know” into a dish you can start cooking, plus ideas for every kind of day.

By the What2Eat team · Published 17 November 2026 · 7 min read

In this article

  2. Ideas by situation

It's late afternoon, someone asks "what's for dinner?" and your mind goes blank. You're not short of recipes. You're short of a decision. The good news is that deciding gets much easier when you stop looking at every possible dish and start answering a few small questions in order.

Here is the method we built What2Eat around. It works with a notebook, with your memory, or with an app.

The five questions that decide a meal

Ask these in this order. Each answer cuts the list of possible dishes down until only a handful are left.

  1. Which meal is it? Breakfast, lunch, evening snack or dinner. Breakfast rules out heavy curries; evening snacks rule out anything that needs rice.
  2. How much time do I have? Be honest. Twenty minutes means eggs, daal, a stir-fry or pasta. Ninety minutes opens up biryani, nihari or a slow roast.
  3. Is today a normal day or a special day? A normal day wants simple homestyle food: daal roti, sabzi, kadhi chawal. A special day (guests, a weekend, a celebration) can handle biryani, karahi, BBQ or pizza night.
  4. What did we eat in the last few days? If you had chicken twice this week, the third chicken dish will get complaints. Pick a different main ingredient.
  5. What's already in the kitchen? The best dish is often the one you can make without a shop run.

By the time you reach question five, you rarely have more than three or four sensible options. Pick one and move on. A good decision made quickly beats a perfect decision made at 8 pm.

Ideas by situation

When you have 20 minutes

  • Masoor daal with jeera rice (red lentils cook fast and need no soaking)
  • Egg bhurji with paratha or toast
  • Vegetable fried rice using yesterday's rice
  • Pasta with a quick tomato and garlic sauce
  • Chicken wraps with leftover chicken and fresh salad

When everyone is tired of the usual

Change the cuisine, not the effort. If you always cook desi food, try a simple Chinese-style stir-fry, a Turkish lentil soup or an Italian baked pasta. The ingredients overlap more than you think: onions, garlic, tomatoes and a protein go a long way in every kitchen.

When it's a special day

Plan one showpiece dish and keep the rest simple. A chicken karahi or pulao with raita, salad and one sweet dish feels like a feast without filling your whole day. Our guide to planning meals for guests covers bigger menus.

When you have random leftovers

Start from the ingredients rather than the dish. Two tomatoes, an onion and some chicken already make a karahi-style curry, a pasta sauce or a stir-fry. See what to cook with ingredients you already have for a full method.

Why "what should I cook" feels so hard

The question shows up two or three times a day, every day. Each time, you're balancing taste, time, budget, nutrition, what's in the fridge and what everyone else will eat. That's a lot of small trade-offs, and doing them repeatedly is tiring. Researchers often call this decision fatigue: the more choices you make, the harder the next one feels.

The fix isn't more recipes. It's fewer open questions. Rules like "Monday is daal day" or "Friday is something special" remove choices before they start. So does keeping a short list of family favourites you can rotate.

Three habits that make tomorrow easier

  1. Write down what you cooked. A quick note on your phone helps you avoid repeats and shows you which dishes your family actually enjoys.
  2. Keep a "yes list". Ten to fifteen dishes everyone likes. On a bad day, pick from the list without thinking.
  3. Decide dinner at breakfast. Choosing early gives you time to defrost meat, soak lentils or buy one missing ingredient.

Quick answers

What's the easiest thing to cook when I can't decide?

Daal and rice, an egg dish with bread, or a one-pot pasta. They're cheap, quick, and most families eat them without complaint.

How do I stop cooking the same thing every week?

Track what you cook and rotate the main ingredient: lentils, chicken, vegetables, beef or mutton, fish, eggs. Changing the cuisine once a week also helps.
