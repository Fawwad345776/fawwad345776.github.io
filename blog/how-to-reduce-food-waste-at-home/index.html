How to reduce food waste at home

Twelve practical habits for planning, shopping, storing and cooking so less food ends up in the bin.

By the What2Eat team · Published 17 November 2026 · 8 min read

In this article

  2. Store food so it lasts
  3. Cook with waste in mind
  4. Leftover makeovers
  5. Understand date labels
  6. Quick answers

Food waste is a bigger household problem than most people think. According to the UN Environment Programme's Food Waste Index Report 2024, the world wasted about 1.05 billion tonnes of food in 2022, and around 60% of it came from households. On average, each person wasted about 79 kg of food at home that year. The report also found that the difference between richer and poorer countries is small, so this isn't only a "rich world" problem.

The good news: most household waste comes from a few habits, and changing them saves money as well as food.

Plan before you shop

  1. Plan your meals for a few days. Even a rough plan stops you buying ingredients "just in case". See the best ways to plan daily meals.
  2. Check the fridge first. Look before you write the shopping list, so you don't buy a second bunch of coriander when the first one is still fresh.
  3. Shop with a list and buy realistic amounts. Bulk deals only save money if you use everything.

Store food so it lasts

  1. First in, first out. Move older items to the front of the fridge and new ones to the back.
  2. Store herbs like flowers. Coriander and mint last longer with their stems in a glass of water, or wrapped in a damp cloth in the fridge.
  3. Keep onions and potatoes apart. Store them separately in a cool, dark, ventilated place.
  4. Freeze before it spoils. Bread, cooked rice, chopped onions, ginger-garlic paste, curries, ripe bananas and tomatoes all freeze well.
  5. Label leftovers. A piece of tape with the date tells you what to eat first.

Cook with waste in mind

  1. Cook from what you have. Before deciding on dinner, look at what needs using up. Our guide on what to cook with ingredients you already have explains how.
  2. Cook the right amount. A cup of uncooked rice usually serves two to three people. Measure instead of guessing.
  3. Plan a leftovers night. One meal a week built from whatever is left: fried rice, wraps, paratha rolls, soups.
  4. Use the whole ingredient. Coriander stems go into chutney, vegetable peels into stock, stale bread into pudding or breadcrumbs.

Understand date labels

"Use by" dates are about safety, so follow them for meat, fish and dairy. "Best before" dates are about quality: many dry goods, snacks and canned foods are still fine to eat after that date if stored well. Check the smell, look and texture, and when in doubt about high-risk foods, throw them out.

Quick answers

What is the easiest way to reduce food waste at home?

Plan meals for a few days and shop with a list. Most waste comes from buying more than you cook.

Which foods are wasted the most at home?

Fresh produce, bread, cooked rice and leftovers are among the most common, because they spoil quickly or are cooked in excess.
