What to cook with ingredients you already have

A simple base-plus-protein method for turning a half-empty fridge into dinner, with combinations that work.

By the What2Eat team · Published 17 November 2026 · 7 min read

Some of the best home meals start with "we don't have anything to cook". Usually that isn't true. There's an onion, a couple of tomatoes, some eggs, a cup of rice, a few potatoes. What's missing is a way to see those ingredients as a dish.

The base, protein, flavour method

Almost every everyday dish is built from three layers. Find one of each in your kitchen and you have a meal.

  1. Base: something filling. Rice, roti or flour, bread, pasta, potatoes, noodles.
  2. Protein: eggs, lentils, chickpeas, beans, chicken, mince, fish, paneer, yoghurt.
  3. Flavour: onion, tomato, garlic, ginger, green chillies, spices, lemon, herbs, soy sauce, cheese.

Then choose a cooking style: curry, stir-fry, bake, soup, or wrap. The same three layers give you very different dishes depending on the style.

A five-minute fridge check

  1. Find what needs using first. Soft tomatoes, wilting greens, cooked rice, opened yoghurt. Build the meal around these.
  2. Pick your protein. If there's no meat, eggs and lentils are almost always there.
  3. Check your aromatics. Onion, garlic and ginger turn plain ingredients into a proper dish.
  4. Choose the style that matches your time: stir-fry and eggs are fastest, curries are medium, baking takes longest.
  5. Accept substitutions. No fresh tomatoes? Use tomato paste or yoghurt for tang. No cream? Use milk with a little butter.

Keep a small "always" pantry

A few long-life staples make it possible to cook something good on any day. A useful list for a desi kitchen: rice, atta, two or three types of lentils, dried or canned chickpeas, onions, garlic, ginger, canned tomatoes or paste, cooking oil or ghee, salt, red chilli, turmeric, cumin, coriander powder, garam masala, eggs and yoghurt. With these, you're never more than half an hour away from daal chawal, an egg curry or a kadhi.

Common mistakes when cooking from what you have

  • Using everything at once. Three or four ingredients cooked well beat ten thrown together.
  • Skipping the base flavour. Take time to brown your onions or bloom your spices in oil. It's what makes the dish taste intentional.
  • Forgetting acid. A squeeze of lemon, a spoon of yoghurt or a little vinegar lifts a flat dish.
