Three ways to stop staring into the fridge

Most days you need one answer. Some days you have guests, or half a bag of onions and two tomatoes. What2Eat has a tool for each.

Meal Decider

Pick breakfast, lunch, evening snacks or dinner. Choose how many main dishes you need and whether it's a normal day (daal roti, sabzi, kadhi chawal) or a special one (biryani, nihari, BBQ, pizza). Add sweets if you like. What2Eat suggests the meal and keeps track of what you've cooked so it doesn't repeat itself.

How the Meal Decider works

Pantry Matcher

Type what's in your fridge, or tap quick-add ingredients like tomatoes, onions, garlic and ginger. Pantry Matcher finds recipes that use them, so leftovers become dinner instead of waste.

More about Pantry Matcher

Guest Planner

Guests on Sunday? Tell the Guest Feast Planner how many main dishes you want (one to seven) and how many sweets. It builds a balanced dawat menu you can tweak dish by dish. For Ramadan, Eid and other celebrations, the Special Occasion menu suggests traditional festive dishes.

Everything else your kitchen asks for

The same app plans your week, translates recipes and helps when nobody wants to cook.

Weekly Planner

A 7-day lunch and dinner schedule shaped by your preferences, with AI auto-suggest. Part of PRO.

Dine Out

Not cooking tonight? See nearby restaurants sorted by top rated or nearest, with directions.

Kitchen Preferences

Tell the app what you want to cook, what you never want suggested and your daily habits.

Recipes in 7 languages

Read any recipe in English, Roman Urdu, Urdu, Hindi, Arabic, Spanish or Bengali.

Community dishes

Add your family's recipe with ingredients, steps and a chef's tip, and browse dishes from other cooks.

Cooked history

Tap "Cooking this today" and the dish is logged, so future suggestions stay fresh.

From question to dinner in four steps

  1. Set your kitchen

    Choose your city and set Kitchen Preferences once.

  2. Choose the moment

    Pick a meal time, number of dishes and a normal or special day.

  3. Get a suggestion

    Swap any dish you don't feel like, or ask for more ideas.

  4. Cook it

    Open the full recipe and tap "Cooking this today".

Built in Pakistan, cooking for everyone

What2Eat started with the everyday desi kitchen: daal chawal on a weekday, karahi when guests come, pakoras at iftar. The library now covers Pakistani, Indian and Bangladeshi cooking alongside Turkish, Emirati, Saudi, Afghan, Chinese, Thai, Japanese, Italian, British, American and Mexican dishes.
